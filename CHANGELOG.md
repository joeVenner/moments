# Changelog

All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Website: returning guests who never received their guest identity (they joined before it existed, or their join failed on the network) now get it in the background, so their nickname stays theirs once identities are required.
- iOS: the app is available in French. Every screen, message and VoiceOver label is translated, with proper plural forms. `npm run ios:strings:check` flags any new app text that has no translation.
- iOS: a "Your uploads" screen for each event shows every photo and video as waiting, uploading (with a percentage), finishing, shared or failed. Fixable failures get "Try again", and finished items can be swiped away. A button in the event's top bar shows how many are under way and flags failures. It appears once uploads are connected in the app.
- Guests can leave an event from the app ("Leave event" in the event menu). It deletes the photos and videos they shared from the app, cancels their unfinished uploads, and frees their nickname. It works even after the event's PIN changed. Anything shared from the website under the same name stays, and the confirmation says so.
- Joining an event is now rate limited per event and network (60 a minute), on both the website and the app, so one network can't create guests in bulk. Guests returning with their saved identity are never limited. A limited join gets "Too many people are joining from this network. Try again in a minute."; the app shows this on the name step, and a returning guest keeps their identity and can try again.
- The iOS app has a screen to share photos and videos: pick up to 30 at once from the photo picker (no photo-library access needed), see each one being prepared (with the reason and Try again if one fails), add an optional caption and share them together. Videos are shared with their sound, and the screen says so. Closing the screen drops anything not shared, and leftover picked originals are cleared at every launch. Hidden until uploads are connected to storage.
- The iOS app can import photos and videos from the photo picker without asking for photo-library access. Each pick is copied into a private, unbacked-up folder, cleaned for upload, and the copy is deleted once it's done (or kept briefly so a failed attempt can be retried). Links, folders and files over 4 GB are refused, and the phone's free space is checked first.
- The iOS app prepares each video before upload. It is re-encoded to at most 1080p (never enlarged, portrait kept upright) in a format every browser plays, and nothing hidden survives: no location, phone model, editing app or capture date. Videos over 10 minutes, and results over the 512 MB upload limit, are refused; cancelling stops the work and leaves no files behind.
- The iOS app prepares each photo before upload. It is resized to at most 2560 pixels, turned upright and saved as a JPEG. All hidden information is removed: location, phone model, capture time and the original color profile. Only common photo formats are accepted, and damaged or oversized files are refused.
- The iOS app can keep sending photos and videos while it is in the background or after the system closes it. After a relaunch it picks up transfers still in progress instead of sending them again. Temporary piece files are deleted when each transfer ends, and leftovers are cleared at launch without touching pieces still being sent.
- The iOS app can send queued photos and videos straight to storage, piece by piece. Pieces only go to Cloudflare R2 storage addresses, over a secure connection, never to a bare IP address or another server. Your guest login is never sent to storage. Storage must confirm each piece it receives, and if some pieces were sent before a failure they count as progress. Temporary piece files are cleared at every launch, so a crash leaves no photo pieces behind. This first version sends while the app is open; background sending comes next.
- The iOS upload queue now runs on its own. After a restart it checks with the server before resending anything. Each upload is opened, sent and finished on the server, and its progress is saved after every step. Retries back off with a little randomness, so phones that failed together don't retry together. A copied file is deleted once its upload is finished. Each upload keeps its copied files in a folder of its own, so one upload can never read or delete another's files. An upload whose file was deleted stops with a clear message. When the phone is locked before its first unlock, uploads wait instead of failing. Not wired to the app yet; the real file sending comes next.
- The iOS upload queue is saved on the phone, one file per upload, so queued photos and videos survive the app being closed. A damaged file is set aside without losing the rest. Files that can't be read yet (before the phone is first unlocked after a restart) wait for a later launch instead of being deleted. Saved files are left out of iCloud backups. Upload files are only read from inside the app's upload folder, and only if they still have their recorded size. Not wired to the app yet.
- iOS upload queue logic (`UploadEngine`): each queued photo or video is a saved record that decides its next step (open the upload, send missing parts, finish, re-check an unclear answer) and how to react to every server answer. Network trouble waits and retries (5 s doubling to 15 minutes, 20 tries; time offline doesn't count). A cancelled upload starts fresh at most twice. A retry never creates a second moment or re-uploads one the host removed, and a server that keeps answering "parts missing" can't make the app upload forever. Saved records hold no upload links and are checked when read back. Not wired to the app yet.
- iOS upload groundwork: the app's API client can open an upload session, check which parts have arrived, get part links, ask the server to finish, and cancel, with a typed result for finished, parts missing, rejected (with why) and cancelled, and typed errors for each upload problem. A planner splits a file into the exact parts the server expects. Part links are presigned write credentials, so they never print, and they must be plain https links matching what was asked for. Nothing uses this yet; the upload queue comes next.
- Server-side upload finishing for the native app: `POST /api/v1/events/{slug}/uploads/{id}/complete` finishes a session (safe to repeat). The server checks that every part R2 holds has exactly the expected size before assembling the file, then checks the file's size, type and first bytes (JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP, HEIC/HEIF, MP4, QuickTime, WebM) and creates the moment with the caption given at the start. Missing parts get `409 upload_incomplete` with the parts R2 has; a mismatch gets `422 upload_rejected`, cancels the session and deletes the file. Sessions now show `completed_at` and `moment_id`. A daily sweep finishes sessions the app never reported (5 per run), and the orphan sweep now also deletes files of sessions it cancels when no moment uses them. The web upload routes refuse to finish or cancel app sessions, so their checks can't be skipped. Each daily cron stage now runs even if an earlier one fails.
- Upload sessions for the native app (API v1, migration 0016): a joined guest opens a session per queued item (a retry after a lost response returns the same session), gets every part URL in one call (valid 6 h), asks which parts R2 already has, and can cancel. Open sessions are capped per guest and per event and rate-limited per guest (`UPLOAD_LIMITER`). Finishing an upload on the server comes next.
- iOS Highlights and Leaderboard tabs: the event screen now has Moments, Highlights and Leaderboard tabs. Highlights shows the most-hearted moments (open them full screen and heart them; hearts stay in step with the feed). The leaderboard shows a podium for the top three and everyone else below, marks you, and throws confetti when you reach the podium (not under Reduce Motion). Both load when shown and on pull to refresh, without polling.
- Highlights and leaderboard for the native app: `GET /api/v1/events/{slug}/highlights` returns the most-hearted visible moments (feed shape, media signed for private events), and `GET /api/v1/events/{slug}/leaderboard` ranks guests by points from visible moments, with shared ranks on ties and each guest's avatar seed. Both need the access grant on private events and are rate limited per network (`GUEST_READ_LIMITER`, 120 a minute).
- iOS reactions: double-tap a photo in the viewer to heart it (a heart burst, a light haptic), or use the heart button with its count; feed cells show heart counts. Hearts change at once and roll back with an error haptic if the server refuses; rapid taps settle on the last one. Double-tap on an unzoomed photo now hearts instead of zooming (pinch, or VoiceOver's Zoom in, to zoom; double-tap a zoomed photo to zoom out). The burst honours Reduce Motion.
- Reactions for the native app: `PUT` and `DELETE /api/v1/events/{slug}/moments/{id}/reaction` add or remove this device's heart and return the moment's total. Repeating either is harmless, so the app can update instantly and retry. Only visible moments of the event can be hearted, private events need their access grant, and once guest tokens are enforced only devices that joined may react. A new `REACTION_LIMITER` rate limit (60 a minute per device and event) slows count inflation. The web's toggle route is unchanged.
- iOS full-screen viewer: tap a moment in the feed to open it full screen. Swipe sideways between moments (older ones keep loading), pinch or double-tap to zoom a photo and drag to look around, swipe down or tap Close to leave, and tap to show or hide the uploader, caption and position. Videos play with sound from their signed link or Stream; a video still processing or failing to play says so. VoiceOver reads each moment as a sentence and offers close and zoom actions; animations follow Reduce Motion.
- Signed media links for the native app: on private events, `/api/v1` feed, changes and event responses give every `/media/` URL (originals, thumbnails, posters, cover) an `at` signature for that one file, valid 6-12 hours, so video players and image loaders can fetch it without the access header. Changing the PIN or re-privatising the event revokes them; hidden moments stay hidden. The web app is unchanged. The iOS image cache keys by path, so a re-signed link reuses the cached image.
- iOS feed screen (`FeatureGuest`): once joined, a guest sees the event's moments in a three-column grid, newest first, with older moments loading as they scroll and pull to refresh. New moments arrive every 15 seconds (and on return to the app) behind a "N new moments" button instead of shifting the grid; hidden moments disappear at once. Loading, empty, offline, ended and access-changed states each get a message ("Join again" for the last). Videos show their poster with a play badge; VoiceOver reads each cell as a whole sentence.
- iOS media loading (`MediaFetcher`, `ThumbnailLoader`): private-event images are fetched with the access grant, sent only to the site itself (never to Stream or another host, never across a redirect). Thumbnails are capped at 15 MB while downloading, refused above 50 megapixels, decoded at cell size, cached within 64 MB, limited to six downloads at once, and dropped when access is lost.
- iOS reactions are marked per device as on the web, but with a different id for each event (derived from a random per-install secret) so events can't be linked to one device.
- iOS join screen (`FeatureGuest` in MomentsKit): an event link opens the event, unlocks a private one with the link's invite or the PIN (wrong PIN, replaced invite, lockout wait and expired access each get their own message), then asks for a name. The guest token, name and access are kept on this device; returning guests are confirmed with the server and go straight in. Losing a name to another device (even before tokens are enforced) is reported as taken.
- iOS link handling (`AppRouting` in MomentsKit): event links, App Clip launches and scanned QR codes open `/e/{slug}` (with its invite) or the slideshow. Only https links on moments-hq.com are accepted; other sites, ports, paths, encoded paths and links with two invites are ignored, and the invite never appears in logs. The app shows which event a link opened (screens come next).
- iOS credential storage (`CredentialStore` in MomentsKit): per-event access grant, guest token and participant kept in the keychain, on this device only, never synced, readable after first unlock so background uploads work. Entries are tied to the event's ID so a reused slug can't pick up another event's credentials, updates are atomic, unreadable entries are never wiped, and a fresh install starts clean.
- Private-event access for the native app: `POST /api/v1/events/{slug}/access` exchanges a PIN or the invite token for a signed grant (no cookie), sharing the web route's lockout and slowdown; `MomentsClient.access(slug:credential:)` returns it with typed errors (wrong PIN or invite, too many attempts with the wait, unavailable).
- iOS API client (`MomentsAPI` in MomentsKit): generated from the v1 OpenAPI document with Apple's swift-openapi-generator (exact-pinned), wrapped in `MomentsClient` with the `X-Client` version header, typed errors that never carry credentials, no redirects or cookies, and mapping into the domain models. Media links are accepted only from the site itself or Cloudflare Stream; a moment this build can't read is skipped instead of failing the feed. `npm run openapi:update` now also refreshes the iOS copy of the spec.
- iOS design system (`DesignSystem` in MomentsKit): the web's dark colour tokens (a test fails if they drift from `src/client/index.css`, others enforce WCAG AA), system-font text styles that follow Dynamic Type, spacing and radii, Reduce Motion-aware animation, and named haptics. The app's placeholder screen uses it.
- iOS app target: `ios/Moments.xcodeproj` (SwiftUI, iOS 17+, Swift 6) with a placeholder screen in the dark theme and a hosted Swift Testing target. Build settings live in `ios/Config/Base.xcconfig`; the bundle prefix (placeholder `com.example`) and signing team go in a git-ignored `ios/Config/Local.xcconfig`.
- iOS groundwork: `ios/MomentsKit`, a local Swift package (Swift 6, iOS 17+). Its `MomentsDomain` module holds the app's pure logic with Swift Testing coverage: strict parsing of the API's UTC timestamps, points and milestones matching the web, domain models for events, guests, moments and media, and a feed state that merges pages and incremental changes without resurrecting hidden moments or skipping updates.
- Events can close uploads before they are deleted: `uploads_close_at` and `purge_after` (migration 0015) both default to `expires_at`, so existing events behave as before. Once uploads close the event stays viewable and joinable; new uploads get `403 uploads_closed`, and uploads already in progress have 30 minutes to finish. Event responses (web and `/api/v1`) carry an `uploads_open` flag, and the admin lifecycle endpoint accepts the two dates.
- `/api/v1` for the native app: `getEvent`, `getFeed`, `getFeedChanges` and `joinEvent` (JSON), validated with Zod. The OpenAPI 3.1 description is served at `/api/v1/openapi.json` and committed as `openapi/v1.json` (`npm run openapi:update` regenerates it). iOS builds below `MIN_IOS_BUILD` get `426`.
- Feed items carry a `media` object (kind, url, thumb/display/poster URLs, Stream `hls_url`, width, height, duration, blurhash). Migration `0013` adds the backing columns; filling them in counts as a change for `/feed/changes`.
- Cursor-based feed for native clients: `GET /api/events/:slug/feed?before=<seq>` pages newest-first, and `GET /api/events/:slug/feed/changes?since=<cursor>` returns new or edited moments plus the ids of hidden or deleted ones. Cursors are per event. The web feed is unchanged.
- Joining an event returns a per-device `guest_token` (send it back as `X-Guest-Token`). The first device to join under a nickname claims it; only the token's SHA-256 hash is stored. Tokens are accepted but not yet required (`GUEST_TOKENS_ENFORCED`).
- The web app stores its guest token per event and sends it when re-joining.
- Route tests can run against the real D1 migrations through a Node SQLite adapter (requires Node 22.13+).
- Private-event unlock now returns a signed access token (`access_token`, `expires_at`) that native clients can send as the `X-Event-Access` header.
- Admin privacy controls warn that saving a new PIN signs every guest out and replaces the invitation link.

### Changed
- Native feed responses report `reacted` as a boolean. The web moment list is unchanged.
- The web moment list and highlights return an explicit set of columns, so internal storage columns are never exposed to guests.
- Nicknames longer than 60 characters are rejected when joining.
- Participant responses no longer use `SELECT *`; they return only public fields.
- New event PINs must be 6 to 20 characters. Existing shorter PINs keep working.
- Setting a new PIN, or making a public event private again, revokes all previously issued access and rotates the invitation link.

### Fixed

- Website: going from one event straight to another no longer carries over the first event's screen state ("not found", the PIN prompt, the open tab, an upload or a join in progress).
- iOS: VoiceOver now says "3 new moments" instead of reading out formatting code when new moments arrive.
- Website: when joining is refused ("too many people are joining from this network" or a taken nickname), the join form now stays open and says why. Before, the guest entered without their guest identity.
- Captions can no longer hide text-direction override characters, which could make a caption read differently than it is (for example a file name that seems to end in .txt). The app refuses them and says so; the website silently removes them. Emoji and Arabic, Persian or Hebrew text are unaffected.
- A purge no longer forgets upload sessions it failed to abort in R2: their rows stay (without guest data) until the daily sweeper aborts them.- Event slugs no longer end in a dash (or contain `--` with a suffix) when a long title is cut at a separator.
- Abandoned multipart uploads are now cancelled in R2, so their parts stop costing storage. A daily job sweeps sessions still open after 7 days, or 30 minutes after an event's uploads close (up to 40 per run). Deleting an event (the daily purge or "archive now") now cancels its unfinished uploads before removing their records, which used to leave billed parts behind.

### Security

- Leaving an event from the app now also removes the guest's hearts in that event, those made with their guest identity, so a guest who leaves leaves no trace.
- Website: photo and video uploads now send the guest's identity with each request to the server (never to storage), ready for identities being required. Nothing changes for guests yet.
- Hearts are now tied to the guest who made them when the guest sends their identity, as the website and the app now do. One guest keeps one heart per moment even if their device's heart id changes; after a reinstall that kept the login, their earlier hearts follow them. The website's heart button now checks the heart id, is rate limited like the app's, and will require joining once identities are required. A double tap no longer errors, and a refused heart leaves the button as it was. Needs migration 0017.
- R2 error responses (which can echo object keys, and so file names) are no longer logged or returned to clients; only the status and S3 error code are.- Hidden moments are no longer served by direct `/media` links: originals and their thumbnail, display and poster versions return 404. Public media is cached for 5 minutes and then revalidated (it was cached for a year), so a hide takes effect quickly. Admin moment responses carry short-lived signed view links (`?mv=`), so moderators can still see hidden and private-event media. Stream videos in public events are not covered by this check.
- Private-event Stream videos can require signed playback URLs. Set `STREAM_SIGNING_KEY_ID` and `STREAM_SIGNING_JWK` to turn it on. New uploads to private events are then locked, every Stream URL returned for a private event carries a 1–2 hour token, and saving an event's access settings locks or unlocks its existing videos. Upload metadata forwarded to Stream is now restricted to an allowlist. Tokens are bearer URLs shared by everyone viewing in the same hour; the only way to revoke them early is deleting the signing key.
- Private-event access uses HMAC-signed, version-bound grants instead of the raw, never-rotated invite token. Requires the `EVENT_ACCESS_SECRET` Worker secret; without it, unlocking fails closed with 503.
- PIN attempts are throttled per event and client (IPv6 grouped by /64, IPs stored only as keyed hashes), with an event-wide slowdown and then block. The invitation link is never throttled.
- Invite-token and PIN-hash comparisons are constant-time.
- Legacy raw-token cookies are accepted only until 2027-01-15, and only for events whose access was never revoked.
